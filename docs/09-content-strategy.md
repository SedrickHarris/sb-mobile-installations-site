## 2. Strategic Content Role

The website content must serve three connected business functions.

### 2.1 Recruit qualified installers

Content should help relevant candidates:

- discover SB Mobile Installations;
- understand the work;
- evaluate company and opportunity credibility;
- determine whether their experience fits;
- understand requirements and practical tradeoffs;
- distinguish active openings from future opportunities;
- complete an application; and
- progress into screening and onboarding.

### 2.2 Establish commercial credibility

Content should help commercial visitors:

- understand verified services;
- determine project fit;
- evaluate process, coverage, and proof;
- understand how installations are planned and documented; and
- submit a qualified project inquiry.

### 2.3 Create an authoritative public record

Content should help search engines, job platforms, business profiles, answer systems, candidates, customers, and partners identify:

- who the company is;
- what it does;
- which entity recruits or contracts;
- which jobs are active;
- which services are verified;
- where opportunities and services apply;
- how the company operates; and
- when information was last reviewed.

## 3. Content Principles

### 3.1 Accuracy before persuasion

Content must be persuasive because it is clear, relevant, and supported, not because it exaggerates.

### 3.2 Candidate quality before application volume

Recruitment content should attract suitable applicants and help unsuitable candidates self-select out. Hiding real requirements may increase submissions but reduce recruiting efficiency.

### 3.3 Useful depth before page volume

Publish fewer strong pages rather than many thin variations.

### 3.4 One intent per primary page

Every page should answer a defined audience need and lead to a relevant next action.

### 3.5 Proof before superlatives

Replace terms such as “best,” “leading,” and “trusted” with specific evidence.

### 3.6 Clear language before jargon

Use industry terms candidates and customers recognize, then explain acronyms and technical concepts when needed.

### 3.7 Real operations before marketing architecture

The content system may plan for jobs, services, technologies, and markets that are not yet public. Planned architecture does not make them active business facts.

### 3.8 Every page needs maintenance ownership

Time-sensitive content must have an owner and review date.

- Expenses and lodging when applicable

- Posted, reviewed, and closing dates

### Writing order

1. State the role and location.
2. Explain the work.
3. State the essential terms.
4. List responsibilities.
5. Separate required and preferred qualifications.
6. Explain tools, vehicle, schedule, travel, and documentation.
7. Explain the application process.
8. Provide company context.

- One approved job record
- Visible content matches structured data
- No keyword-stuffed title
- No false location
- No expired opportunity
- No network-only resume collection presented as a job
- No unsupported compensation or classification
- Closing workflow assigned

For question-led content:

1. State the question in a natural heading.
2. Answer it directly in one concise paragraph.
3. Add conditions or limitations.
4. Expand with steps, lists, tables, or examples.
5. Link to the relevant role, service, or action.

### Useful answer formats

- Definitions
- Required-versus-preferred tables
- Ordered processes
- Comparison tables
- Checklists
- Short FAQs
- Decision criteria

### Rules

- Answers must stand on their own.
- Do not write false certainty.
- Do not duplicate the same answer across many pages.
- Do not guarantee featured snippets or People Also Ask placement.

- where privacy information is available.

### Field labels

- Use visible labels.
- Use examples only as supporting help.
- Explain required formats.
- Distinguish required and optional fields.
- Avoid judgmental qualification language.

### Errors

- Identify the field and problem.
- Explain how to correct it.
- Preserve submitted data where practical.
- Never imply success after a failed submission.

### Confirmation

- Confirm the actual submission type.
- Identify the job or network path.
- Explain approved timing and next steps.
- Provide a legitimate help route.

## 38. Image and Media Content

- Authentic installation work
- Real equipment and vehicle environments
- Installer hands, tools, and documentation where appropriate
- Team and leadership portraits

### Recruitment media goals

- Show the work accurately.
- Help candidates understand field conditions.

- Humanize the company.
- Support installer stories.

### Commercial media goals

- Show installation context.
- Demonstrate equipment, vehicle, and documentation detail.

### Rules

- Do not present stock or AI imagery as company work.
- Do not show unsafe practices.

- Do not imply platform partnerships through visible branding.
- Use captions and alt text based on actual image purpose.

## 40. Content Production Workflow

1. Identify business need and audience.
2. Confirm source-of-truth status.
3. Validate search intent and target URL.
4. Review existing and competitor content.
5. Interview the appropriate subject-matter expert.
6. Create the content brief.
7. Draft the content.
8. Complete factual and editorial review.
9. Complete recruiting, operational, privacy, or legal review as required.
10. Add approved media, metadata, links, and structured data.
11. Build or update the page.
12. Review in the Cloudflare preview environment.
13. Complete accessibility, mobile, SEO, form, and content QA.
14. Approve publication.
15. Approve indexation separately.
16. Measure performance and downstream quality.
17. Refresh, consolidate, redirect, or retire based on evidence.

## 44. Content Calendar Strategy

### Launch phase

Prioritize foundational pages over recurring articles.

### First 90 days after launch

Recommended sequence:

#### Month 1

- Publish and optimize verified active jobs.
- Review candidate questions and form abandonment.
- Publish one role-definition resource.
- Publish one qualification resource.
- Complete employer-profile consistency work.

#### Month 2

- Publish one transferable-skills resource.
- Publish one travel or assignment resource.
- Improve FAQs from recruiter feedback.
- Publish or strengthen one verified commercial service page.

#### Month 3

- Publish one technical or documentation resource.
- Publish one commercial planning resource.
- Add approved installer proof.
- Review market-specific recruiting needs.
- Consolidate or improve weak pages.

### Ongoing cadence

Set cadence based on subject:

- active hiring needs;
- expert availability;
- content quality;
- candidate questions;
- commercial priorities;
- performance data; and
- maintenance capacity.

Do not commit to a volume that lowers accuracy or usefulness.

## 46. Content Refresh Strategy

### Active jobs

- Monitor continuously.
- Review on assigned dates.
- Close immediately when no longer available.

### Careers evergreen pages

- Review quarterly during active recruiting.
- Update when terms, process, roles, or systems change.

### Services and service areas

- Review at least twice yearly.
- Update immediately when capability or coverage changes.

### Technology pages

- Review when platforms, hardware, permissions, or relationships change.

### Resources

- Review annually or sooner when facts, search intent, or company processes change.

### Legal and privacy

- Review when forms, vendors, data use, consent, law, or company structure changes.

### Refresh actions

- Confirm facts.
- Update answers.
- Add proof.
- Remove obsolete claims.
- Improve internal links.
- Update visible dates honestly.
- Review metadata and schema.
- Consolidate overlapping pages.
- Redirect or retire content that no longer serves a purpose.

## 47. Content Quality Scorecard

Score each major page from 0 to 2 on each factor.

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business truth | Unsupported | Partially verified | Fully approved |
| Audience fit | Unclear | Broadly relevant | Precisely relevant |
| Search intent | Mismatched | Partially satisfied | Fully satisfied |
| Original value | Generic | Some unique value | Strong first-party value |
| Clarity | Confusing | Understandable | Direct and easy to scan |
| Proof | None | Limited | Relevant approved evidence |
| Conversion | No action | Generic action | Intent-matched action |
| Internal linking | Isolated | Basic links | Strong contextual relationships |
| Accessibility | Significant issues | Minor issues | Meets project standard |
| Freshness | Stale | Review needed | Current and owned |

### Recommended threshold

Do not intentionally index a strategic page below 16 out of 20 without a documented exception.

This score is a project management tool, not a search ranking formula.

## 50. Content QA Checklist

Before publication, confirm:

- Primary audience is clear.
- Primary intent is satisfied.
- Opening paragraph establishes context.
- Business facts are approved.
- Job status and terms are current.
- Hiring or contracting entity is correct.
- Required and preferred qualifications are separated.
- Service scope is precise.
- Platform relationships are described accurately.
- Geographic language is verified.
- Title, description, H1, and URL align without duplication.
- Headings are logical.
- Technical terms are explained.
- Claims have appropriate evidence.
- Internal links are relevant and working.
- Primary CTA matches intent.
- Forms and confirmation messages are accurate.
- Structured data matches visible content.
- Images are accurate and approved.
- Alt text and captions are useful.
- Mobile layout is readable.
- Accessibility requirements are met.
- Author, reviewer, and dates are accurate where displayed.
- Publication and indexation are intentionally approved.
- Maintenance owner and review date exist.

## 51. Prohibited Content Practices

- Fabricating jobs, customers, reviews, results, partnerships, certifications, or statistics
- Presenting the installer network as an active job
- Leaving expired jobs live with active markup
- Mass-generating city pages
- Publishing AI drafts without human review
- Copying competitor wording
- Keyword stuffing
- Hiding text
- Creating multiple pages for trivial keyword variants
- Publishing platform logos as implied endorsements
- Presenting technician experience as a company partnership
- Using stock or AI imagery as proof of company work
- Guaranteeing rankings, rich results, knowledge panels, featured snippets, or AI citations
- Publishing employment or contractor terms without approval
- Collecting unnecessary sensitive candidate data
- Using vague calls to action that conceal what happens next
