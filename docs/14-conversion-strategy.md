## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the SB Mobile Installations website will turn relevant visitors into qualified installer candidates, installer-network members, referral partners, and commercial opportunities.

It governs:

- conversion journeys;
- calls to action;
- page-level conversion roles;
- application and inquiry forms;
- candidate self-qualification;
- trust and objection handling;
- mobile conversion;
- confirmation and follow-up;
- CRM routing;
- source attribution;
- funnel measurement;
- experimentation;
- privacy and consent boundaries; and
- conversion quality governance.

The strategy prioritizes meaningful downstream outcomes over button clicks, raw leads, or unqualified application volume.

## 2. Outcome Hierarchy

### Primary business outcome

Attract, qualify, and activate installers who fit verified SB Mobile Installations opportunities.

### Supporting recruitment outcomes

- Increase qualified organic and referral candidate discovery.
- Help candidates understand the work before applying.
- Improve application starts and completions without hiding requirements.
- Reduce applications from clearly unsuitable candidates.
- Preserve job and source context through recruiting operations.
- Build a permission-based installer network for future needs.
- Shorten the time from application to human follow-up.

### Secondary commercial outcome

Generate qualified conversations with organizations that have a verified need for the company's approved installation capabilities.

### Supporting commercial outcomes

- Clarify service and project fit.
- Build operational credibility.
- Capture the information needed for useful follow-up.
- Route inquiries to a separate commercial pipeline.
- Connect search and profile activity to opportunities.

## 3. Conversion Principles

### 3.1 Candidate quality before application volume

The system should attract suitable candidates and help unsuitable candidates self-select out. Hiding travel, location, classification, tools, scheduling, physical, or experience requirements may raise submission volume while lowering recruiting performance.

### 3.2 Clarity before persuasion

Visitors should understand what the opportunity or service is, whether it fits, what information is required, and what happens next.

### 3.3 One primary conversion per page

Each page must have one dominant action aligned with its search intent and funnel stage.

### 3.4 Separate journeys

Candidate applications, installer-network registrations, referrals, and commercial inquiries require distinct forms, confirmations, CRM pipelines, automations, permissions, and success metrics.

### 3.5 Low friction with necessary qualification

Collect only what is needed at the current stage. Use progressive disclosure instead of asking every possible question at first contact.

### 3.6 Truth is a conversion feature

Accurate job, location, travel, compensation, classification, service, and process information builds trust and prevents costly downstream mismatches.

### 3.7 Mobile is the primary application environment

Job discovery, application, resume handling, validation, and confirmation must work comfortably on a phone.

### 3.8 Measure through the operational outcome

The primary recruitment metric is not an application. It is a qualified, approved, and activated installer. The primary commercial metric is not a form submission. It is a qualified opportunity and resulting business value.

## 6. Recruitment Funnel

### Stage 1: Discovery

The candidate finds the company through search, a job platform, a referral, social content, a technical school, a veteran network, or an installer community.

### Stage 2: Relevance

The page confirms:

- role;
- location or territory;
- travel expectations;
- work environment;
- active versus evergreen status; and
- fit with the candidate's experience.

### Stage 3: Evaluation

The candidate reviews:

- responsibilities;
- requirements;
- preferred experience;
- tools or vehicle expectations;
- schedule and classification when approved;
- compensation when approved or required;
- company credibility; and
- application process.

### Stage 4: Application

The candidate starts and submits a job-specific application through a secure, mobile, accessible flow.

### Stage 5: Confirmation

The system confirms successful receipt, identifies the application or job context, explains realistic next steps, and prevents unnecessary duplicate submissions.

### Stage 6: Qualification

Recruiting reviews the candidate against approved criteria. No public form or automated score makes an unreviewed final employment or contracting decision.

### Stage 7: Screening and evaluation

Qualified candidates proceed through the approved phone, technical, interview, documentation, or other process.

### Stage 8: Approval and onboarding

The responsible organization completes the approved engagement and onboarding workflow.

### Stage 9: Activation

The installer reaches the defined operational activation milestone. This is the primary recruitment outcome.

## 7. Installer-Network Funnel

The installer network is a future-opportunity pathway, not an active job.

### Funnel

1. Candidate confirms that no matching opening is being represented.
2. Candidate reviews who should join and how information may be used.
3. Candidate provides a limited qualification profile.
4. Candidate selects communication preferences and consent.
5. System confirms network registration, not application receipt or work availability.
6. Recruiting reviews and segments the record.
7. Candidate may be contacted when a relevant verified opportunity arises.

### Required disclosures

- Joining does not constitute an application for an active job.
- Joining does not guarantee contact, assignment, interview, employment, contracting, schedule, volume, or compensation.
- The candidate can update or withdraw information through the approved method.

## 8. Commercial Funnel

### Stage 1: Discovery

The buyer finds a service, technology, industry, solution, service-area, case-study, profile, or company page.

### Stage 2: Fit

The page clarifies the approved capability, customer type, project conditions, coverage, and limitations.

### Stage 3: Trust

The buyer evaluates process, documentation, experience, proof, business identity, and contact credibility.

### Stage 4: Inquiry

The buyer provides enough project context for a useful first response without completing an excessive questionnaire.

### Stage 5: Qualification

The commercial owner confirms service, technology, volume, location, schedule, access, and decision fit.

### Stage 6: Discovery or estimate

The company completes the approved commercial discovery, scope, or estimate process.

### Stage 7: Opportunity outcome

The inquiry becomes a qualified opportunity, proposal, won project, lost opportunity, or disqualified record with a documented reason.

## 10. Mixed-Intent Routing

Terms such as “GPS installer,” “telematics installer,” and “mobile installer” can describe a technician or a service provider.

### Routing rules

- Clarify the page's intended audience near the top.
- Use distinct labels such as “Installer Careers” and “Fleet Installation Services.”
- Give the primary audience the dominant CTA.
- Offer the secondary journey through a discreet text link or separate card.
- Never combine candidate and commercial forms.
- Do not send commercial visitors to an application route.
- Do not send applicants to the quote pipeline.
- Preserve the selected journey in analytics and CRM routing.

The homepage may present both pathways, but recruitment remains visually and structurally primary.

## 12. CTA Copy Standards

CTA labels must describe the action and destination.

### Strong labels

- Apply for This Opening
- View Current Openings
- Join the Installer Network
- Review Role Requirements
- Discuss Your Fleet Installation Project
- Check Verified Service Areas

### Weak labels

- Submit
- Click Here
- Learn More
- Get Started
- Continue
- Send

Generic labels may be acceptable inside a clearly labeled multistep form, but primary page CTAs should name the action.

### Rules

- Do not label a network registration as an application.
- Do not label an information request as a quote if no quote process exists.
- Do not use artificial urgency.
- Do not promise response times without operational approval.
- Do not use “free quote” without approval.
- Keep the label consistent before and after navigation.

## 13. CTA Placement

Important pages should present the primary action at natural decision points.

### Recommended positions

- Hero or opening viewport
- After the primary fit summary
- After requirements or scope
- After process and proof
- Near the final page conclusion
- Mobile sticky action on high-intent job pages when it does not obstruct content

### Rules

- Repeated CTAs should lead to the same relevant action.
- Do not stack several competing buttons in one area.
- Do not add a button when an existing nearby action already serves the same purpose.
- Do not interrupt reading with aggressive popups.
- Preserve visible focus, accessible labels, and sufficient touch targets.
- Do not let a sticky CTA cover form controls, legal text, or browser controls.

## 22. Application Architecture

Possible implementations:

1. Embedded application on the active job page
2. Shared `/careers/apply/` form with a validated job ID
3. Approved external applicant-tracking form

### Preferred principle

Use the least disruptive secure flow that preserves:

- canonical job context;
- verified job ID;
- source attribution;
- candidate privacy;
- accessible mobile behavior;
- secure file handling;
- error recovery;
- CRM or ATS integration; and
- successful-submission confirmation.

Do not select a form method solely because it is easy to embed.

## 23. Progressive Application Model

A staged application balances completion and qualification.

### Step 1: Opportunity and contact

- Job title and validated job ID
- Candidate name
- Email
- Phone
- Home city and state or approved territory field
- Preferred contact method when needed

### Step 2: Core fit

- Relevant experience category
- Years or level of experience using approved ranges
- Relevant equipment or skill categories
- Travel availability
- Schedule or territory eligibility when material
- Required job-specific screening questions

### Step 3: Experience detail

- Resume upload when required or useful
- Plain-text experience summary alternative when appropriate
- Certifications or credentials only when verified as relevant
- Referral source

### Step 4: Review and consent

- Application summary
- Ability to correct entries
- Required acknowledgments
- Privacy notice
- Email and SMS consent separated where required
- Final Apply for This Opening action

The final step count should reflect the approved application system and candidate testing. Do not split a short form into unnecessary screens.

## 24. Minimum Initial Application Fields

The initial application should generally collect only what is required to identify, contact, and perform first-stage qualification.

### Recommended baseline

- Validated job ID
- Name
- Email
- Phone
- Home market
- Relevant experience summary or structured categories
- Travel availability when relevant
- Resume or alternative experience summary
- Referral source
- Required consent

### Do not collect initially

- Social Security number
- Bank or payment information
- Tax documents
- Identity-document images
- Full background-check information
- Date of birth unless a lawful, approved stage requires it
- Medical or disability details
- Unnecessary demographic data
- Customer-confidential details from prior work
- Passwords or account credentials

Later-stage data collection requires an approved need, secure system, access controls, retention rule, and appropriate notice.

## 25. Screening Question Standards

Questions should predict job fit, not merely increase form length.

### Appropriate subjects when verified

- Relevant installation experience
- 12-volt or automotive-electrical experience
- GPS, ELD, telematics, or approved equipment familiarity
- Vehicle types worked on
- Testing and documentation experience
- Tools or vehicle readiness when legitimately required
- Travel and territory availability
- Schedule availability
- Job-specific credential or legal requirement

### Rules

- Distinguish required from preferred.
- Explain uncommon terms.
- Use answer options that reflect real qualification decisions.
- Provide an accessible “other” or explanation path when needed.
- Do not ask unlawfully discriminatory or unnecessary personal questions.
- Do not auto-reject based on an unreviewed model inference.
- Obtain legal or HR review for sensitive eligibility questions.

## 26. Resume and File Upload

### Requirements

- State whether upload is required or optional.
- Provide accepted file types and size limits before selection.
- Support common mobile file sources.
- Scan or otherwise protect against malicious uploads.
- Store files outside the public web root.
- Restrict access by role.
- Use nonpredictable storage identifiers.
- Do not place the original filename in public URLs or analytics.
- Confirm successful upload before submission.
- Provide a text alternative when operationally acceptable.
- Apply retention and deletion rules.

Do not send resumes as unsecured public links or expose them in application confirmation URLs.

---

## 27. Form Field Standards

- Use visible persistent labels.
- Mark required and optional fields clearly.
- Use the correct input type and autocomplete token.
- Provide examples as help, not as the only label.
- Use concise field-level instructions.
- Group related fields with semantic markup.
- Preserve entered values after recoverable errors.
- Avoid unnecessary dropdowns for short option lists.
- Allow pasting into contact fields.
- Do not block password managers or accessibility tools.
- Use reasonable input formatting rather than rigid masks.
- Avoid asking for the same information twice.

---

## 28. Validation and Error Recovery

### Client-side validation

Provide immediate helpful feedback for basic format and required-field issues.

### Server-side validation

Treat the server or trusted form platform as authoritative. Revalidate all fields, job status, job ID, file type, rate limits, and consent records.

### Error messages

- Identify the affected field.
- Explain the problem in plain language.
- Explain how to correct it.
- Move focus to an error summary when appropriate.
- Preserve safe submitted data.
- Never announce success after failure.
- Provide a legitimate recovery route for repeated errors.

### Closed-job race condition

If a job closes while a candidate is completing the form:

- stop the submission safely;
- explain that the opening is no longer accepting applications;
- preserve data only according to approved policy;
- offer the installer network with separate consent; and
- do not silently convert the application into a network registration.

---

## 29. Mobile Conversion Standards

- Use a single-column form layout.
- Keep labels and errors close to controls.
- Use appropriate mobile keyboards.
- Keep touch targets large enough for reliable use.
- Avoid horizontal scrolling.
- Minimize repeated typing.
- Preserve progress when the browser changes apps for a file or verification step.
- Keep pages and forms fast on cellular connections.
- Avoid intrusive overlays.
- Test with common phone sizes and browsers.
- Ensure sticky CTAs do not cover controls or content.
- Make phone and email actions explicit.

The active job and application flow receive the highest mobile-performance priority.

---

## 30. Accessibility Conversion Standards

Conversion flows must meet the project's accessibility standard.

At minimum:

- full keyboard operation;
- visible focus;
- programmatic labels;
- semantic field grouping;
- accessible error identification;
- status announcements for dynamic steps;
- no color-only meaning;
- sufficient contrast;
- zoom and reflow support;
- accessible file-upload controls;
- no unexpected context changes; and
- clear timeout behavior if a session can expire.

Accessibility defects are conversion defects and release blockers for critical forms.

## 34. Human Follow-Up Strategy

Conversion does not end at form submission.

### Recruitment

- Assign an accountable recruiting owner.
- Define a realistic first-review service level.
- Send a receipt confirmation from the correct entity.
- Preserve role and source context.
- Prioritize candidates using approved criteria.
- Record contact attempts and outcomes.
- Provide consistent status language.
- Stop job-specific automation when the role closes.

### Commercial

- Assign an accountable commercial owner.
- Confirm service and project fit.
- Route by service, market, volume, and urgency where supported.
- Record contact attempts and qualification outcomes.
- Prevent applicant records from entering sales automation.

Do not publish speed claims until the responsible team can meet them consistently.

---

## 35. Email and SMS Follow-Up

### Confirmation messages

Confirm the action the user actually completed and identify the represented entity.

### Consent

- Separate required transactional notices from optional marketing or future-opportunity messages.
- Record consent language, time, source, and method.
- Honor opt-out and communication preferences.
- Do not infer SMS consent from providing a phone number.
- Obtain legal review for applicable email, texting, and recruiting rules.

### Message rules

- Do not imply acceptance, hiring, work, assignment, pricing, or availability.
- Do not include sensitive candidate or project data unnecessarily.
- Use verified sender domains and reply paths.
- Monitor delivery failures.
- Stop or update sequences when status changes.

## 36. Closed and Paused Opportunity Conversion

When a job is filled, paused, canceled, or expired:

1. Stop the job-specific application.
2. Remove it from active job listings.
3. Remove or expire `JobPosting` markup.
4. Update job-board listings.
5. Stop job-specific ads and automations.
6. Apply the approved archive, redirect, `404`, or `410` treatment.
7. Offer current openings or installer network only with accurate language.
8. Preserve source and disposition records as required.

Do not redirect every closed job directly to a generic application form. Do not silently treat prior applicants as applicants for a new job.

## 43. Ethical Persuasion and Urgency

### Allowed

- Real application deadline
- Verified limited opening count
- Actual project timing constraint
- Clear consequence of incomplete information
- Honest next-step explanation

### Prohibited

- Fake countdown timers
- False “limited spots” language
- Unverified “hiring immediately” claims
- Preselected consent
- Forced newsletter signup
- Misleading button labels
- Confirm-shaming
- Hidden requirements
- Manufactured scarcity
- Automatic network enrollment after an application
- Making optional data appear required

Urgency must come from real circumstances, not interface pressure.

---

## 44. Privacy and Consent

Before any form launches, document:

- receiving entity;
- processing purpose;
- required versus optional fields;
- form processor;
- CRM or ATS;
- resume and file storage;
- access roles;
- subprocessors;
- retention and deletion;
- communication use;
- withdrawal or correction method;
- incident-response owner; and
- applicable legal review.

### Interface requirements

- Link the relevant privacy notice before submission.
- Use plain-language consent.
- Do not bundle optional marketing or future-opportunity consent into required application acknowledgment.
- Do not place sensitive information in URLs, analytics, or client-side logs.
- Do not expose application status publicly.

---

## 45. Security, Spam, and Fraud Controls

### Form protections

- Server-side validation
- Rate limiting
- Bot detection that does not create unnecessary accessibility barriers
- Honeypot or equivalent low-friction controls
- File validation and malware protection
- Secure transport
- Secret management
- Restricted webhook endpoints
- Duplicate-submission controls
- Audit logs appropriate to the risk

### Fraud communication

When approved, tell candidates:

- which domains or contact methods the company uses;
- that legitimate recruiting will not request inappropriate payment or sensitive financial data at the initial application stage; and
- how to verify suspicious communication.

Do not publish a fraud warning that makes claims about processes not yet approved.

## 47. Attribution Strategy

Preserve source context across website, application, CRM, and downstream outcome.

### Capture when available

- First landing page
- Latest landing page
- Referrer
- UTM source, medium, campaign, and content
- Recruitment platform
- Job ID
- Role category
- Market
- CTA location
- Device category
- Consent state

### Rules

- Use lowercase controlled values.
- Validate job IDs and campaign parameters.
- Do not place personal or sensitive data in URLs.
- Keep canonical URLs free of tracking parameters.
- Preserve first-touch and last-touch separately when possible.
- Record “direct,” “unknown,” and offline referral honestly.
- Avoid overwriting original attribution during application steps.

## 50. Qualification and Scoring

Scoring may support prioritization but must remain explainable, validated, and subordinate to human review.

### Recruitment factors

- Required experience match
- Relevant technical skills
- Market eligibility
- Travel availability
- Schedule fit
- Required tools, vehicle, licenses, or credentials when lawful and verified
- Completeness of application

### Commercial factors

- Approved service fit
- Technology or equipment fit
- Vehicle or asset volume
- Market coverage
- Timeline feasibility
- Decision authority
- Project information completeness

### Rules

- Document each factor and weight.
- Do not infer protected or sensitive characteristics.
- Do not use opaque AI scoring for final decisions.
- Review false positives and false negatives.
- Keep qualification separate from marketing engagement scores.
- Allow an appropriate human override with a reason.

## 52. Funnel Diagnostics

| Symptom | Likely questions | Investigation |
|---|---|---|
| High job views, low apply clicks | Is fit or trust unclear? Are requirements unattractive or confusing? | Page recordings, candidate interviews, CTA visibility |
| High starts, low completions | Is the form too long, broken, or asking for hard-to-find information? | Step abandonment, field errors, mobile testing |
| High completions, low qualification | Are real requirements hidden or screening questions weak? | Source, job copy, qualification reasons |
| Qualified candidates not contacted | Is ownership or response capacity failing? | CRM timestamps and alerts |
| Many network registrations, few usable profiles | Is the network proposition too broad? | Criteria, form fields, source quality |
| Commercial traffic, few inquiries | Is service fit, proof, or CTA unclear? | Landing intent and objection review |
| Many inquiries, few opportunities | Is targeting too broad or qualification too weak? | Service, market, and source analysis |
| Confirmation views exceed submissions | Can users access the success URL directly? | Server-side event and route controls |

## 53. Experimentation Framework

### Appropriate tests

- CTA label or placement
- Long page versus anchored navigation
- Embedded versus shared application flow
- One-page versus progressive form
- Resume required versus approved alternative
- Proof module placement
- Requirements summary format
- Job-card information hierarchy
- Mobile sticky CTA behavior
- Commercial form grouping

### Do not test

- Different job facts for different users
- Hidden compensation, classification, or travel terms
- Fake urgency
- Preselected consent
- Misleading success messages
- Reduced accessibility
- Different privacy disclosures
- Removing required legal information
- Auto-rejection without approved human review

### Test requirements

1. State the hypothesis.
2. Identify the primary metric and guardrails.
3. Define the eligible audience.
4. Confirm no business fact changes.
5. Run long enough for a meaningful sample.
6. Evaluate downstream quality, not only clicks.
7. Document the decision.

## 59. Risk Register

| Risk | Impact | Control |
|---|---|---|
| High application volume but poor fit | Recruiting overload | Clear requirements and qualification |
| Form too long on mobile | Candidate abandonment | Progressive flow and field reduction |
| Wrong entity receives application | Legal and trust risk | Entity and routing gate |

## 62. Definition of Done

The launch conversion foundation is complete when:

- recruitment is the unmistakable primary website journey;
- active jobs, installer network, referrals, and commercial inquiries use separate paths;
- every priority page has one intent-aligned primary CTA;
- candidates can understand material job requirements before applying;
- the application flow is secure, accessible, mobile-first, and tested;
- job ID and source survive through submission and CRM;
- closed jobs cannot accept new job-specific applications;
- network registration does not imply an active job or guaranteed work;
- commercial inquiries cannot enter the recruiting pipeline;
- confirmations represent verified successful submissions;
- privacy, consent, storage, retention, and access are approved;
- human owners receive and act on submissions;
- analytics exclude personal information;
- downstream qualification, screening, opportunity, and activation outcomes can be measured; and
- the team can identify and correct conversion failures quickly.

## 63. Maintenance

Review this strategy:

- when roles, markets, terms, services, entities, or response capacity change;
- when the form, ATS, CRM, file storage, email, SMS, or analytics system changes;
- when privacy, consent, accessibility, or employment requirements change;
- when candidate or commercial feedback reveals friction;
- after meaningful changes in source quality or conversion rate;
- after any form, routing, data, or automation incident;
- before launching a new conversion path;
- during quarterly funnel reviews; and
- whenever raw conversion growth does not produce qualified applicants, activated installers, or commercial opportunities.

Update page copy, CTAs, forms, schema, analytics, CRM fields, automations, privacy notices, QA checks, and reporting together when a conversion decision changes materially.
