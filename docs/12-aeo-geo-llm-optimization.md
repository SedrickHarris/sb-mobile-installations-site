## 1. Purpose

This document defines how SB Mobile Installations will make its public information easy for traditional search engines, answer engines, generative search systems, and large-language-model search experiences to discover, understand, verify, cite, and connect to a useful next action.

It governs:

- answer engine optimization (AEO);
- generative engine optimization (GEO);
- LLM-search visibility;
- featured-snippet and People Also Ask readiness;
- entity clarity;
- topical authority;
- citation-worthiness;
- crawler access;
- structured data;
- source corroboration;
- AI-assisted editorial work;
- platform measurement; and
- conversion from answer experiences.

This plan does not replace SEO. It extends the project's technical SEO, content, local, schema, and conversion standards into answer-led discovery environments.

## 2. Working Definitions

### Search engine optimization

Improving crawlability, indexation, relevance, quality, authority, usability, and conversion for search experiences.

### Answer engine optimization

Structuring accurate content so a system can identify and present a useful answer to a specific question.

### Generative engine optimization

Improving the clarity, evidence, authority, and accessibility of content that may be selected or cited in generative search responses.

### LLM-search optimization

Preparing public content for discovery and use by search experiences that use language models, web indexes, live retrieval, third-party search providers, or combinations of those systems.

### Citation

A visible reference or link from an answer system to an SB Mobile Installations page. A brand mention without a source link is tracked separately.

### Answer unit

A self-contained section that answers one defined user question with sufficient context, qualification, evidence, and a relevant next step.

### Entity

A distinct person, organization, service, role, technology, industry, location, or other concept that systems can identify and relate to other entities.

## 6. Core Principles

### 6.1 SEO remains the foundation

Google states that its established SEO practices continue to apply to generative search. Crawlable, indexable, helpful, reliable, well-structured content remains the baseline.

### 6.2 Optimize for people and retrieval together

Write for a candidate or buyer who needs a decision, while making the answer easy for a system to locate and interpret.

### 6.3 Be quotable because the information is useful

Use concise definitions, direct answers, original operational detail, verified facts, and clear limitations. Do not manufacture unnatural text fragments for bots.

### 6.4 Evidence beats repetition

First-party process detail, real examples, named reviewers, source links, current dates, and consistent entity data are stronger than repeating keywords or questions.

### 6.5 One source of truth

The website, job platforms, business profiles, directories, schema, social profiles, and public documents must not tell different versions of the company story.

### 6.6 No visibility guarantee

Crawl access, indexation, quality, and platform compliance create eligibility. They do not guarantee rankings, citations, featured answers, knowledge panels, or inclusion.

### 6.7 Conversion remains the business test

An answer citation is useful only when it improves qualified discovery, candidate quality, commercial opportunity, trust, or operational efficiency.

## 11. Answer Unit Specification

Every important question should be answered through a complete content unit.

### Required elements

1. **Question or descriptive heading**
2. **Direct answer** in the opening sentences
3. **Scope** defining who, what, or where the answer covers
4. **Qualification** explaining conditions, exceptions, or uncertainty
5. **Evidence** from approved first-party knowledge or authoritative sources
6. **Supporting detail** appropriate to the decision
7. **Related entity links** for deeper context
8. **Next action** aligned with the visitor's intent
9. **Owner and review date** when the answer is time-sensitive

An answer unit must make sense when encountered through search, a citation preview, or a deep link. It must not require the reader to infer critical context from a previous section.

## 12. Direct-Answer Pattern

Use a concise opening answer when the question permits one.

### Pattern

> **Answer:** [Direct response]. [Critical qualification]. [Useful implication or next step].

### Recruitment example

> A mobile telematics installer installs, connects, tests, and documents approved fleet technology at customer sites, fleet yards, or shops. Exact duties, travel, and equipment vary by verified SB Mobile Installations opportunity, so candidates should review the active job page before applying.

### Commercial example

> Mobile fleet installation brings qualified installation support to the vehicles or operating sites involved in a deployment. Availability, equipment scope, vehicle types, and market coverage must be confirmed for each SB Mobile Installations project.

Examples are editorial patterns, not approved operational claims beyond the business source of truth.

## 13. Heading and Question Standards

- Use an H1 that clearly names the page topic.
- Use H2 and H3 headings to describe real subtopics.
- Use question headings when users genuinely ask the question.
- Do not convert every heading into a question.
- Keep each section focused on one concept.
- State acronyms with their full term on first meaningful use.
- Use parallel heading language for comparisons and processes.
- Avoid vague headings such as “Learn More” or “Solutions.”
- Do not repeat near-identical questions to target variants.

Readable organization supports users first. It is not a special generative-search markup system.

## 17. FAQ Standards

FAQs are an editorial format, not a shortcut to rankings or rich results.

### Publish an FAQ when

- the audience repeatedly asks the question;
- an approved answer exists;
- the answer reduces friction or confusion;
- the question fits the page's primary intent; and
- the answer adds information not already clear nearby.

### Do not publish

- invented questions;
- duplicate keyword variants;
- unsupported employment or service claims;
- “contact us” in place of an available answer;
- promotional answers disguised as neutral information; or
- FAQ structured data without current eligibility.

The visible FAQ answer and any structured data must match.

## 21. Source and Citation Standards

### External citations

- Link to the original or most authoritative source.
- Place the citation near the supported claim.
- Use descriptive anchor text.
- Confirm the source actually supports the statement.
- Record access or review dates for changing requirements.
- Replace obsolete sources.
- Avoid citation chains that only point to other summaries.

### Internal evidence

- Link to the canonical entity or process page.
- Identify first-party experience honestly.
- Separate public evidence from confidential internal records.
- Maintain an editorial source note when public linking is not appropriate.

Outbound links should help the reader verify or act. They are not endorsements unless explicitly stated.

## 22. Factual Confidence Labels

During drafting, classify claims as:

| Status | Meaning | Publication rule |
|---|---|---|
| Verified | Approved by the responsible owner with adequate evidence | May publish |
| Conditionally approved | Accurate only under named conditions | Publish with qualification |
| Proposed | Strategically useful but not approved as fact | Internal planning only |
| Inferred | Reasonable interpretation not confirmed by owner | Do not publish as fact |
| Disputed | Conflicting sources or stakeholders | Resolve before publication |
| Expired | Previously accurate but no longer current | Remove or update |

AI systems can amplify ambiguity. Unverified claims must not enter public content merely because the prose sounds plausible.

## 30. Crawl and Indexation Foundation

An answer system cannot reliably retrieve content that is inaccessible, blocked unintentionally, unstable, or rendered only after fragile interactions.

### Requirements

- Public canonical URLs return `200` responses.
- Important content is present in rendered HTML.
- Navigation and internal links use crawlable anchors.
- XML sitemaps list canonical indexable URLs.
- Robots rules reflect approved business policy.
- Canonical tags are accurate.
- Noindex directives are deliberate.
- JavaScript, CSS, and media required to understand the page are accessible.
- Cloudflare security rules do not block approved search crawlers.
- Redirects are limited and intentional.
- Soft 404s, duplicate pages, and parameter traps are controlled.
- Structured data can be rendered and validated.

Search access and model-training permission are separate policy decisions.

## 37. `llms.txt` Policy

Do not make `llms.txt` a launch requirement.

Google explicitly states that it does not use `llms.txt` for Google Search, including its generative AI features. Other organizations may publish their own `llms.txt` files for documentation discovery, but that does not establish a universal ranking or citation standard for business websites.

If the team later tests an `llms.txt` file:

- treat it as experimental;
- do not expose private or unapproved content;
- do not let it conflict with canonical HTML;
- do not use it instead of sitemaps, links, metadata, or structured data;
- record the hypothesis and success criteria;
- monitor bot behavior and citations; and
- remove it if it adds maintenance risk without measurable value.

## 40. Internal Linking for Answer Discovery

Internal links define relationships among answers and entities.

### Requirements

- Use descriptive anchor text.
- Link from pillar pages to supporting questions.
- Link supporting pages back to their pillar.
- Link role resources to current jobs and installer network.
- Link active jobs to qualifications, process, privacy, and application pages.
- Link services to relevant technologies, industries, markets, proof, and inquiry paths.
- Link definitions to the canonical detailed page.
- Avoid orphan pages.
- Avoid sitewide links to every page.
- Do not use vague anchors when the destination can be named.

Internal links must reflect real relationships, not every theoretically possible matrix combination.

---

## 41. Off-Site Corroboration

Answer systems may compare the website with external sources. Build consistent, legitimate corroboration through:

- Google Business Profile;
- Bing Places;
- Apple Business and Apple Maps;
- controlled social profiles;
- recruitment platforms;
- industry associations;
- trade schools and workforce partners;
- veteran employment networks;
- verified technology or business partners;
- reputable directories; and
- earned media or event participation.

Do not purchase fake mentions, create unowned profiles, distribute conflicting descriptions, or publish an active job on one channel after it has closed elsewhere.

---

## 42. Reputation and User-Generated Evidence

Authentic customer and candidate experiences can influence trust, but different evidence types must remain distinct.

### Customer reviews

- Request only from genuine customers.
- Do not gate, incentivize, or script reviews.
- Respond without exposing private information.
- Use approved testimonials only with permission.

### Installer and candidate stories

- Obtain written consent.
- Identify whether the person is an employee, contractor, installer-network member, or applicant when relevant and approved.
- Do not present one person's experience as a universal promise.
- Do not move recruiting testimonials into commercial-review systems.

### Community discussion

Monitor public questions and misconceptions for research. Do not seed deceptive discussions or impersonate unaffiliated users.

---

## 43. Content Freshness

Freshness must reflect real review and change, not cosmetic date updates.

### High-frequency review

- Active jobs
- Compensation or engagement terms
- Application steps
- Contact information
- Recruiting markets
- Commercial service areas
- Platform and crawler requirements

### Scheduled review

- Role and qualification guides
- Services and technologies
- Process documentation
- FAQs
- Author biographies
- Sources and outbound links
- Schema eligibility

Display `dateModified` only when a meaningful public revision occurred. Record internal review dates separately when no visible content changed.

---

## 44. AI-Assisted Content Workflow

Claude, ChatGPT, or another approved model may assist with research organization, outlining, drafting, comparison, QA, and repurposing. A model is not the source of business truth.

### Required workflow

1. Define audience, intent, and business outcome.
2. Assemble approved first-party facts and authoritative sources.
3. Mark unknown or disputed claims.
4. Create the content brief.
5. Draft with source boundaries.
6. Verify every factual claim.
7. Obtain operational, recruiting, legal, or technical review as required.
8. Check originality and usefulness.
9. Validate links, metadata, schema, and conversion paths.
10. Record owner and review date.
11. Publish deliberately.
12. Measure and update.

### Prohibited use

- Inventing quotes, credentials, customers, jobs, pay, locations, certifications, or statistics
- Paraphrasing competitors too closely
- Publishing model output without human verification
- Scaling city or keyword pages without distinct value
- Generating fake reviews, questions, or community posts
- Presenting synthetic imagery as company proof
- Hiding AI-generated factual uncertainty

---

## 45. Scaled Content Controls

Google warns that generating many pages without user value may violate scaled-content-abuse policies, regardless of whether humans or automation created them.

Every proposed page must pass:

- verified business relevance;
- distinct search intent;
- distinct audience need;
- original or first-party value;
- sufficient approved facts;
- unique conversion purpose;
- maintenance ownership; and
- publication and indexation review.

Service, technology, industry, location, role, and audience matrices are opportunity maps. They do not authorize automatic page generation.

---

## 46. Practices Not Required or Not Supported

Do not treat the following as proven requirements for AI visibility:

- keyword density targets;
- arbitrary word counts;
- converting every heading into a question;
- writing unnaturally short “AI chunks”;
- an `llms.txt` file as a universal discovery signal;
- custom “AI schema” not supported by recognized specifications;
- hidden summaries for bots;
- fake expert quotations;
- mass-produced FAQ pages;
- manufactured brand mentions;
- prompt injection in public page copy;
- copying current AI answers and publishing them as original content; or
- guarantees of citation, featured snippets, or knowledge panels.

Useful formatting is encouraged for people. It should not be sold internally as a platform exploit.

---

## 47. Answer-Experience Conversion Design

Visitors arriving from answer systems may already understand the basics. Landing pages should support the next decision quickly.

### Recruitment destination

Show near the top:

- role or topic confirmation;
- active versus evergreen status;
- location and travel context;
- core fit requirements;
- primary apply or view-opening action; and
- installer-network alternative when appropriate.

### Commercial destination

Show near the top:

- service or problem confirmation;
- customer and project fit;
- verified scope and coverage;
- proof or process signal;
- primary inquiry action; and
- relevant qualification information.

Do not force the visitor to reread a generic introduction before reaching the cited answer or next step.

## 48. Measurement Framework

### Search-platform measurement

- Google Search Console search and generative-AI performance reports when available
- Bing Webmaster Tools search and AI Performance reports when available
- IndexNow submission and discovery status
- Crawl and indexation diagnostics
- Structured-data validity
- Profile and local discovery metrics

### Analytics measurement

- Referral source and landing page
- Engaged sessions from AI and search sources
- Job views and apply clicks
- Application starts and completions
- Installer-network submissions
- Commercial calls and forms
- Qualified applicant and lead rates
- Assisted conversions

### Citation monitoring

Track a controlled prompt set across approved platforms:

- whether SB Mobile Installations appears;
- whether the company is described accurately;
- whether a source link appears;
- which URL is cited;
- whether the cited page is current;
- which competitors or sources appear;
- whether the answer changes by location; and
- whether the visit converts.

Treat manual AI-result testing as directional. Results may vary by model, date, conversation, account, location, and retrieval source.

## 50. Prompt-Test Library

Maintain a versioned set of realistic prompts.

### Recruitment examples

- What does a mobile telematics installer do?
- What experience is useful for a GPS installer job?
- Can 12-volt installation skills transfer to fleet telematics work?
- What tools do mobile fleet installers use?
- How much travel can field installer work require?
- Are there active SB Mobile Installations installer jobs in `[market]`?
- How do I apply to SB Mobile Installations?
- What is the difference between an active job and the installer network?

### Commercial examples

- What is mobile fleet technology installation?
- How should a fleet prepare vehicles for telematics installation?
- How are multi-location fleet installations coordinated?
- What testing and documentation should follow installation?
- Does SB Mobile Installations serve `[market]`?
- Who provides mobile fleet installation for `[approved use case]`?

Do not publish or test unapproved location, compensation, certification, customer, or technology claims.

## 51. Evaluation Scorecard

Score each priority answer from 0 to 2.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Wrong or unsupported | Partially supported | Fully verified |
| Directness | No answer | Delayed or vague | Immediate and clear |
| Completeness | Critical gaps | Adequate | Decision-ready |
| Evidence | None | General support | Strong first-party or primary support |
| Entity clarity | Ambiguous | Mostly clear | Explicit and consistent |
| Freshness | Expired | Review unclear | Current and owned |
| Source selection | No citation | Weak destination | Canonical useful destination |
| Conversion | No next step | Generic next step | Intent-aligned next step |

Any accuracy score of `0` is a release blocker regardless of total score.

## 52. Prepublication QA Checklist

### Intent and answer

- [ ] One primary audience and question are defined
- [ ] Direct answer appears early
- [ ] Scope and qualifications are clear
- [ ] General information is separated from company-specific claims
- [ ] Page offers a relevant next action

### Truth and evidence

- [ ] Business facts match the source of truth
- [ ] Active-job facts match the job registry
- [ ] Entity and location language is approved
- [ ] Technical claims have a qualified reviewer
- [ ] External sources are authoritative and current
- [ ] Original evidence is approved for public use

### Search and retrieval

- [ ] Canonical URL is correct
- [ ] Page is intentionally indexable or noindex
- [ ] Title, H1, and description are unique
- [ ] Core content is rendered in HTML
- [ ] Internal links are crawlable
- [ ] Sitemap inclusion is correct
- [ ] Structured data matches visible content
- [ ] Production WAF allows approved crawlers

### Quality

- [ ] Content provides value beyond common summaries
- [ ] No keyword stuffing or artificial question repetition
- [ ] No unsupported AEO or GEO tactic was added
- [ ] Author or reviewer is accurate
- [ ] Dates reflect real publication or modification
- [ ] Accessibility and mobile behavior pass QA

### Conversion

- [ ] Recruitment and commercial CTAs are not confused
- [ ] Application or form destination works
- [ ] Source attribution is captured
- [ ] No sensitive information enters analytics parameters

## 53. Automated and Technical Checks

Where practical, CI or monitoring should detect:

- missing or duplicate metadata;
- noncanonical sitemap URLs;
- broken internal and external links;
- orphan pages;
- invalid JSON-LD;
- expired `JobPosting` data;
- active job pages without required fields;
- indexable utility or confirmation pages;
- blocked production crawlers;
- incorrect status codes;
- redirect chains;
- empty or client-only primary content;
- missing review dates on time-sensitive pages;
- conflicting entity names; and
- accidental publication of placeholders.

Automated checks support review. They cannot verify business truth or usefulness by themselves.

## 54. Implementation Phases

### Phase 0: Resolve business truth

- Confirm public, legal, service, and hiring entities.
- Verify services, technologies, markets, roles, and active jobs.
- Assign operational and editorial reviewers.
- Approve crawler and training-use policies.

### Phase 1: Technical eligibility

- Implement canonical URLs, robots rules, and XML sitemaps.
- Verify Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- Configure IndexNow.
- Test Cloudflare access for approved crawlers.
- Establish schema templates and validation.

### Phase 2: Core answer foundation

- Publish the organization, careers, jobs, services, about, contact, and policy pages.
- Define core entities and acronyms.
- Add direct answers to primary conversion pages.
- Establish internal topic relationships.

### Phase 3: Recruitment authority

- Publish verified role and qualification resources.
- Answer application, travel, tools, testing, and work-environment questions.
- Connect resources to active jobs and installer network.
- Distribute canonical job facts to recruiting platforms.

### Phase 4: Commercial authority

- Publish verified service, technology, industry, solution, and market content.
- Add first-party process assets and approved case studies.
- Strengthen external corroboration and relevant links.

### Phase 5: Measurement and improvement

- Establish prompt baselines.
- Monitor search, AI citations, and crawl logs.
- Measure qualified candidates and inquiries.
- Improve weak answers and destination pages.
- Retire inaccurate or low-value content.

## 56. Risk Register

| Risk | Impact | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong hiring entity cited | Legal and candidate trust risk | Resolve entity before publication |
| Expired job appears in an answer | Candidate harm and policy risk | Expiration automation and monitoring |
| Generic AI content scaled across topics | Low quality and search-policy risk | Page eligibility and first-party-value tests |
| Search bot blocked by Cloudflare | Lost discovery eligibility | WAF and crawler-log validation |
| Training crawler confused with search crawler | Unintended content-use policy | Provider-specific policy matrix |
| AI system cites outdated URL | Wrong user destination | Redirect, canonical, update, and removal controls |
| Recruiting market presented as office | Misrepresentation | Geographic taxonomy and content review |
| Structured data contradicts copy | Search trust and feature loss | Single data record and automated validation |
| Fake expertise or evidence | Reputation and compliance harm | Named reviewer and source records |
| Citation count replaces business outcomes | Misallocated effort | Qualified conversion KPIs |
| `llms.txt` treated as required | Maintenance burden and false confidence | Experimental-only policy |
| Manual prompt tests overinterpreted | Bad strategy decisions | Versioned samples and directional reporting |

## 59. Definition of Done

The initial AEO, GEO, and LLM-search foundation is complete when:

- business and hiring entities are approved;
- priority questions map to canonical pages;
- primary recruitment and commercial pages answer their core questions directly;
- important facts have named sources and reviewers;
- entity relationships are consistent across copy, schema, profiles, and job platforms;
- public content is crawlable and intentionally indexable;
- approved search crawlers can reach the production Cloudflare deployment;
- model-training policies are documented separately from search access;
- sitemaps, IndexNow, and webmaster tools are configured;
- active jobs and expiration controls are working;
- structured data matches visible content;
- citation and prompt baselines are recorded;
- analytics separate recruitment and commercial outcomes; and
- the team can measure qualified conversions rather than only answer visibility.

## 60. Maintenance

Review this plan:

- when Google, Bing, OpenAI, Perplexity, Apple, or another approved platform changes its guidance;
- when crawler user agents, IP ranges, or Cloudflare security policies change;
- when the business or hiring entity changes;
- when new roles, services, technologies, industries, or markets are approved;
- before launching a large content expansion;
- after an inaccurate AI answer creates candidate or customer risk;
- after significant citation, crawl, or indexation changes;
- during quarterly content and technical audits; and
- when visibility is not producing qualified applicants or commercial opportunities.

Update the source of truth, content briefs, entity records, schema, robots policy, WAF rules, sitemaps, platform profiles, measurement plan, and QA checks together when a material decision changes.
