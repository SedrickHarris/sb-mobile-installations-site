## 1. Purpose

This document defines the priority audiences for the SB Mobile Installations website and translates their needs into content, search, user-experience, and conversion requirements.

It is intended to guide:

- website architecture;
- page priorities;
- recruitment content;
- commercial content;
- search-intent research;
- application and inquiry forms;
- calls to action;
- recruitment-platform messaging;
- CRM segmentation;
- email and text-message follow-up;
- analytics events;
- proof collection; and
- conversion optimization.

The personas are strategic models, not claims about every candidate or customer. Assumptions must be tested through stakeholder interviews, applicant data, customer interviews, search data, and post-launch performance.

Factual business, employment, contractor, compensation, service, and coverage claims remain governed by `01-business-source-of-truth.md`.

---

## 2. Audience Strategy

The website serves two distinct audience systems:

1. **Recruitment audiences:** people evaluating current or future installer opportunities.
2. **Commercial audiences:** organizations evaluating installation capability or a business relationship.

Recruitment is the immediate priority. Candidate content, navigation, forms, confirmation pages, follow-up workflows, and analytics must remain distinct from commercial lead generation.

### 2.1 Primary website outcome

Convert relevant visitors into qualified applicants or permission-based members of the installer network.

### 2.2 Secondary website outcome

Convert qualified commercial visitors into project, quote, or partnership inquiries while strengthening company credibility for candidates.

### 2.3 Audience-priority rule

When page space or development resources are limited, prioritize the audience closest to the project’s immediate recruitment objective without removing essential commercial credibility.

## 4. Recruitment Persona 1: Experienced Telematics Installer

### Profile

An experienced technician who has installed GPS, ELD, telematics, fleet-management, camera, tracking, or related connected-vehicle equipment.

### Likely background

- Current or former telematics installer
- Independent mobile technician
- Fleet-technology field technician
- Contractor for an equipment provider or installation network
- Vehicle-electronics specialist
- Technician familiar with commercial fleet environments

### Primary goals

- Find credible, well-defined installation opportunities
- Understand compensation and payment timing
- Identify available markets and travel expectations
- Determine equipment and technical fit
- Evaluate assignment consistency and project organization
- Avoid wasting time on vague or expired listings

### Primary questions

- Is this opportunity currently active?
- Who is hiring or contracting with me?
- Which equipment will I install?
- Where is the work located?
- Is travel required?
- How are assignments offered and scheduled?
- What tools, vehicle, smartphone, insurance, or licenses are required?
- Is compensation hourly, daily, weekly, per unit, or project based?
- Are travel and lodging reimbursed?
- What documentation is required?
- How and when are installers paid?
- Is any amount of work guaranteed?

### Frustrations and objections

- Generic listings without exact territory or role details
- Conflicting compensation or classification information
- Recruiters who respond slowly
- Unclear travel and expense policies
- Unpaid administrative work or excessive documentation
- Poor equipment coordination
- Assignments canceled without clear communication
- Listings that imply guaranteed work without evidence
- Long applications before basic terms are disclosed

### Trust signals

- Accurate active-opening page
- Clear entity and engagement type
- Specific technical requirements
- Plain-language assignment process
- Transparent compensation and expense information when approved
- Authentic installation imagery
- Installer testimonials
- Clear recruiter contact and response expectations
- Professional application confirmation
- Consistent information across website and job platforms

### Likely search behavior

- telematics installer jobs
- GPS installer jobs
- ELD installer jobs
- fleet technology installer jobs
- mobile installer contractor opportunities
- traveling telematics installer jobs
- 12-volt fleet installer jobs
- named equipment or platform plus installer jobs
- role plus city, state, region, or travel modifier

### Preferred content

- Current openings
- Detailed job pages
- Installer requirements
- How assignments work
- Equipment and experience expectations
- Travel and expense FAQ
- Application steps
- Installer testimonials
- Company and project credibility information

### Primary conversion

Apply for a verified active opening.

### Secondary conversion

Join the installer network when no suitable opening is active.

### Messaging direction

Lead with the role, market, equipment context, engagement type, and practical expectations. Avoid generic “exciting opportunity” language.

## 5. Recruitment Persona 2: 12-Volt and Mobile-Electronics Technician

### Profile

A technician with vehicle-electrical experience from mobile electronics, alarms, stereo systems, accessories, cameras, upfitting, or related 12-volt work who may be able to transition into fleet-technology installation.

### Likely background

- Mobile-electronics installer
- Car-audio or alarm technician
- Vehicle-accessory installer
- Upfit technician
- Automotive-electrical technician
- Independent 12-volt contractor

### Primary goals

- Apply existing vehicle-electrical skills to new work
- Determine whether prior experience transfers
- Understand equipment-specific training
- Find opportunities with clear technical expectations
- Expand into commercial fleet or telematics projects

### Primary questions

- Does my 12-volt experience qualify?
- Which wiring and diagnostic skills are required?
- Do I need previous GPS or ELD experience?
- Is training available for specific equipment?
- Must I provide my own tools and vehicle?
- Will I work independently or with a lead technician?

- What types of vehicles will I work on?

### Frustrations and objections

- Job descriptions that demand platform experience without explaining alternatives
- Unclear distinction between preferred and required skills
- Claims of training without details
- Roles labeled entry-level that require extensive independent work
- Unclear testing or certification requirements

### Trust signals

- Skills-transfer explanation
- Required-versus-preferred qualification table
- Realistic description of vehicle access and wiring work
- Approved training or onboarding information
- Equipment-category overview
- Clear technical evaluation process
- Authentic examples of documentation and work standards

### Likely search behavior

- 12-volt installer jobs
- mobile electronics installer jobs
- automotive electrical technician jobs
- car audio installer career change
- GPS installer training jobs
- telematics installer with 12-volt experience
- fleet electronics technician jobs

### Preferred content

- Mobile installer role overview
- Transferable skills guide
- Installer requirements
- Technical FAQ
- Training information, if verified
- Application qualification checklist

### Primary conversion

Apply for an opening that accepts relevant transferable experience.

### Secondary conversion

Join the installer network and identify equipment or training gaps.

### Messaging direction

Show how relevant vehicle-electrical skills connect to the role while remaining explicit about mandatory telematics, diagnostic, documentation, and independent-work requirements.

An experienced installer who is willing and able to travel for multi-day, regional, or multi-state projects.

- Find well-coordinated projects
- Understand project duration and travel area
- Confirm travel, lodging, mileage, and expense terms

- Project-specific details

- Clear cancellation or rescheduling expectations

### Likely search behavior

- traveling installer jobs
- traveling telematics technician jobs

## 9. Recruitment Persona 6: Emerging Technician

### Profile

An early-career candidate with technical aptitude, education, military experience, or introductory vehicle-electrical skills who needs a genuine training and supervision path.

### Likely background

- Technical-school graduate
- Automotive program graduate
- Veteran or transitioning service member
- Apprentice technician
- Entry-level mobile-electronics installer
- Hobbyist seeking professional work

### Primary goals

- Start a technical career
- Gain fleet-technology experience
- Understand training and certification requirements
- Find a realistic path from learning to independent work
- Avoid roles marketed as entry-level that require advanced experience

### Primary questions

- Is this role actually entry-level?
- What experience is required before applying?
- Is training paid or unpaid?
- How long is training?
- Who supervises initial work?
- What tools must I purchase?
- What qualifications are required to work independently?
- Are certifications provided or expected?

### Frustrations and objections

- Misleading entry-level language
- Unpaid or undefined training
- Immediate independent-work expectations
- Large tool expenses without disclosure
- No clear advancement or qualification process

### Trust signals

- Verified training curriculum
- Clear prerequisites
- Defined supervision
- Realistic readiness milestones
- Transparent tool costs
- School or veteran-program relationships
- Clear statement when no entry-level opportunity is available

### Likely search behavior

- entry level installer jobs
- GPS installer training
- 12-volt technician apprenticeship
- automotive electrical trainee jobs
- veteran field technician careers
- telematics installer certification

### Preferred content

- Training page
- Entry requirements
- Career-path explanation
- FAQs
- School or veteran partner information
- Relevant active openings

### Primary conversion

Apply for a verified trainee or entry-level opening.

### Secondary conversion

Subscribe to approved future-opportunity communication if no eligible role exists.

### Messaging direction

Target this persona only when the business can support the promised training and supervision. Do not create entry-level messaging merely to expand reach.

## 10. Recruitment Persona 7: Passive Installer-Network Candidate

### Profile

A qualified installer who is not ready to apply for a current role but is open to future local, regional, or traveling opportunities.

### Primary goals

- Register interest without completing a long application
- Specify markets, skills, and travel availability
- Control future contact preferences
- Learn about relevant opportunities when they arise

### Primary questions

- Is there an active job near me?
- What happens after I join?
- How often will I be contacted?
- Is joining an application or contract?
- Can I update or remove my information?
- Will my information be shared?

### Frustrations and objections

- Talent pools presented as guaranteed work
- Excessive messaging
- Unclear privacy practices
- Repeated requests for the same information
- No ability to update availability

### Trust signals

- Clear future-opportunity disclosure
- Concise form
- Consent choices
- Privacy information
- Update and unsubscribe method
- Accurate explanation of next steps

### Primary conversion

Join the installer network with explicit consent.

### Messaging direction

Clearly state that registration is not an employment offer, contract, active assignment, or guarantee of future work.

## 24. Messaging Principles

### Recruitment messaging should

- identify whether an opening is active;
- distinguish a job application from installer-network registration;
- state the verified entity and engagement type;
- explain work and requirements directly;
- distinguish mandatory from preferred qualifications;
- disclose practical tradeoffs;
- set response and next-step expectations;
- respect experienced technicians;
- avoid inflated opportunity claims; and
- prioritize fit and quality over volume.

### Commercial messaging should

- define verified services precisely;
- focus on project and operational outcomes;
- explain process and accountability;
- distinguish coverage from physical locations;
- support claims with evidence;
- avoid unsupported platform relationships; and
- make project fit easy to evaluate.

### Messaging for all audiences should

- use plain language;
- avoid generic superlatives;
- keep contact information consistent;
- identify SB Mobile Installations clearly;
- answer the primary question early;
- provide a relevant next step; and
- reflect current operating reality.

## 25. Accessibility and Inclusion Considerations

The recruitment experience should:

- work on mobile devices;
- use plain, readable language;
- support keyboard navigation;
- use accessible labels and error messages;
- avoid requiring a resume when an experience summary is sufficient, if approved;
- explain required technology or document formats;
- offer a reasonable contact path for application assistance;
- avoid unnecessary demographic or sensitive-data collection;
- use inclusive language tied to actual job requirements; and
- publish approved equal-opportunity or accommodation information when applicable.

Accessibility does not require lowering technical or safety requirements. It requires presenting legitimate requirements clearly and providing an accessible application process.

## 26. Persona Validation Plan

Validate these personas using:

- interviews with company leadership;
- interviews with recruiting and scheduling personnel;
- interviews with active or former installers;
- review of qualified and unqualified applicant patterns;
- candidate questions and objections;
- application abandonment data;
- source-to-activation reporting;
- recruitment-platform performance;
- Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster query data;
- website search and behavior data;
- customer interviews;
- sales inquiry and proposal data; and
- post-project or post-assignment feedback.

### Validation questions

- Which persona produces the strongest activated-installer rate?
- Which qualification signals predict successful onboarding and assignments?
- Which questions repeatedly delay applications?
- Which terms create confusion or low-quality applications?
- Which channels produce qualified candidates by market?
- Which pages influence completed applications?
- Why do qualified candidates withdraw?
- Which promises or process gaps damage trust?
- Which commercial audiences create the most valuable opportunities?
- How does installer capacity affect commercial conversion?

## 27. Analytics Segmentation

At minimum, reporting should distinguish:

- candidate versus commercial visitor;
- active-opening applicant versus installer-network registrant;
- job ID and market;
- candidate persona or skill category where lawfully and practically inferred from submitted experience;
- device type;
- organic, direct, referral, paid, social, email, and job-platform source;
- individual recruitment platform;
- application start and completion;
- qualification outcome;
- onboarding outcome;
- activation outcome;
- commercial inquiry type; and
- qualified commercial opportunity outcome.

Analytics should not collect unnecessary sensitive candidate information or make automated employment decisions.
