## 1. Purpose

This document defines how SB Mobile Installations will build an accurate, durable local-search presence across its website, search platforms, maps, directories, reviews, and market-specific content.

The plan supports two distinct journeys:

1. **Recruitment:** Help qualified installers discover verified opportunities in markets where the company is actively recruiting.
2. **Commercial:** Help fleet, equipment, logistics, and multi-location organizations discover relevant installation capabilities in markets the company genuinely serves.

Local visibility must be built from verified business facts. A recruiting market, technician location, temporary project site, or national travel capability does not automatically establish a customer-facing business location.

---

## 2. Strategic Position

Local SEO is not a system for manufacturing city relevance. It is a system for connecting accurate business entities, eligible locations, real service areas, useful market content, authentic reputation signals, and measurable conversion paths.

The program must:

- establish a consistent business identity;
- verify location and service-area eligibility before publication;
- separate physical locations, commercial service areas, project markets, and recruiting markets;
- connect controlled profiles to the correct website destinations;
- earn customer reviews without incentives or review gating;
- publish market pages only when they provide distinct local value;
- support discovery in Google, Bing, Apple Maps, and answer systems;
- improve qualified candidate and commercial conversions; and
- create an auditable process for maintaining local information.

Local visibility, profile features, rankings, map placement, rich results, featured snippets, knowledge panels, and AI citations are eligibility-based outcomes. They cannot be guaranteed.

## 5. Local Search Principles

### 5.1 Accuracy before reach

Do not widen stated coverage, create extra profiles, add keyword modifiers to the business name, or publish city pages merely to expand visibility.

### 5.2 One real entity record

The website, profiles, schema, directories, legal content, and customer communications must describe the same approved business entity.

### 5.3 Relevance, distance, and prominence

Google states that local results are primarily based on relevance, distance, and prominence. The plan can improve relevance and prominence through accurate data, useful content, reviews, links, and engagement. It cannot remove the user-to-business distance factor.

### 5.4 Local proof over location repetition

Original market information, verified work examples, local operating details, customer evidence, and useful answers are stronger than repeating a city name.

### 5.5 Separate search intent

Commercial local intent and candidate job intent require different pages, calls to action, analytics, and maintenance workflows.

### 5.6 Owner-controlled assets

SB Mobile Installations should own primary accounts and grant Sirius Systems or another approved partner manager access. Credentials must not depend on an individual contractor's personal account.

## 6. Location and Market Taxonomy

Every geographic record must have one approved classification.

| Geographic type | Definition | Website use | Profile eligibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical customer-facing location | Permanent, staffed location where customers may visit during stated hours | Location page, contact data, directions | Potentially eligible after verification |
| Service-area business base | Legitimate operational base that serves customers at their locations and does not receive customers | Service-area page when justified; address hidden publicly where required | Potentially eligible after verification |
| Hybrid location | Eligible staffed location that receives customers and also serves them elsewhere | Location and service-area content | Potentially eligible after verification |
| Commercial service area | Market where the company verifiably accepts and fulfills customer work | Market page when distinct demand and proof exist | Does not independently justify a profile |
| Recruiting market | Area where a verified role or installer need exists | Active job or qualified evergreen recruiting page | Does not justify a commercial profile |
| Project market | Temporary area associated with a specific deployment | Usually not public; case study only if approved | Not a permanent location |
| Technician territory | Area an installer covers from a mobile base | Operational data; sometimes job content | Technician home is not a company location |
| Future market | Proposed expansion area | Internal planning only | Not eligible until operationally real |

The geographic registry must record status, evidence, owner, last verification date, and approved public language.

## 7. Local Eligibility Audit

Before changing any platform, complete an eligibility audit for each possible location.

### Required evidence

- Legal business name and registration
- Approved public business name
- Permanent operating address
- Lease, utility, registration, or other verification evidence when required
- Exterior and interior business evidence when applicable
- Signage consistent with the represented business
- Customer-access status
- Staffed hours
- Primary phone controlled by the business
- Business email and domain ownership
- Service-area model
- Customer transaction or service evidence
- Existing listing URLs and ownership status
- Duplicate, suspended, or unverified profile history

### Eligibility questions

1. Does the business make in-person contact with customers during stated hours?
2. Is the location permanent and operated by the represented business?
3. Is it staffed by the business during stated hours?
4. Is it a real operational base rather than a mailbox, virtual office, temporary site, or lead-generation location?
5. Should the address display publicly, or should it be hidden for a service-area business?
6. Does this location represent SB Mobile Installations or a different legal entity?
7. Is there another profile for the same business and location?
8. Does the website support the same facts?

If evidence is incomplete, mark the record `pending` and do not create or optimize the profile.

## 9. Google Business Profile Strategy

### 9.1 Initial action

Perform a discovery audit before creating a new profile:

- search by business name, address, phone, previous name, and domain;
- inspect Google Search and Maps results;
- identify claimed, unclaimed, duplicate, moved, closed, or suspended profiles;
- record profile IDs, URLs, owners, categories, address behavior, and review history;
- request owner access through the approved process; and
- resolve duplicates or ownership conflicts before major edits.

Do not create a second profile simply because access to an existing eligible profile is inconvenient.

### 9.2 Ownership model

- SB Mobile Installations retains primary ownership.
- Sirius Systems receives manager access required for approved work.
- At least two company-controlled administrators should be documented.
- Use role-based business accounts when available.
- Store recovery procedures and ownership history securely.
- Remove access promptly when a manager no longer requires it.

### 9.3 Business name

Use the approved real-world public business name. Do not add city names, services, recruiting terms, phone numbers, hours, taglines, or keywords unless they are genuinely part of the represented name.

### 9.4 Category selection

Choose the most specific available category that describes the primary customer-facing business. Additional categories must describe material current services, not keywords, aspirations, installer skills, or customer industries.

Category decisions require live platform review because available categories change.

### 9.5 Address and service area

- Use a precise real-world address for verification.
- Hide the address if customers are not served there and platform rules require service-area presentation.
- Do not use P.O. boxes, remote mailboxes, virtual offices, technician homes, customer sites, or temporary project locations as public company locations.
- Use only specific, accurate service areas.
- Google currently permits up to 20 service areas and advises that the overall boundary generally remain within about two hours' driving time from the business base. Verify the rule before implementation.
- National travel capability must not be represented as a local service area if it conflicts with actual operations or platform rules.

### 9.6 Contact information

- Use a monitored business-controlled phone.
- Use the canonical HTTPS website URL.
- Add campaign parameters to the website URL only if the platform accepts them cleanly and canonicalization remains correct.
- Ensure calls, messages, and form submissions reach the right commercial workflow.
- Do not route applicants into the customer-sales pipeline.

### 9.7 Description

The description should state:

- what the company does;
- who it serves;
- the verified operating model;
- meaningful differentiators supported by evidence; and
- relevant service-area context.

Do not use unsupported superlatives, promotional offers, misleading coverage, URLs, or job-posting copy in the core business description.

### 9.8 Services

Add only approved customer-facing services. Use plain-language descriptions aligned with the website service pages. Installer capabilities observed in recruiting materials do not automatically qualify as commercial offerings.

### 9.9 Hours

- Publish accurate customer-contact or staffed hours.
- Maintain special and holiday hours.
- Do not claim 24-hour availability without operational support.
- Assign one owner for scheduled-hours reviews.

### 9.10 Photos and video

Prioritize authentic assets:

- current logo;
- approved cover image;
- legitimate work vehicles or equipment;
- installation process details that do not expose customer data;
- team or workplace images with consent;
- exterior and interior location evidence when eligible;
- completed-work context; and
- service-area-relevant imagery.

Do not use stock imagery to imply a facility, fleet, employee, project, certification, or customer relationship that does not exist.

### 9.11 Posts and updates

Use profile updates for meaningful customer-facing news, service education, project insights, safety reminders, or approved company announcements.

Recruiting announcements may link to verified active job pages when appropriate, but the profile must not become a substitute for the careers hub or job-distribution system.

### 9.12 Questions and answers

Monitor user questions and answer factually. Use recurring questions to improve website FAQs and service content. Do not seed deceptive user-generated questions or publish confidential operational details.

### 9.13 Profile suspension prevention

High-risk changes require review before submission:

- business name;
- address;
- category;
- ownership;
- major service-area changes;
- domain or phone changes; and
- profile consolidation.

Keep an evidence packet and change log for every material profile update.

## 13. Commercial Market Page Eligibility

Publish `/service-areas/[market]/` only when all required conditions are met.

### Required conditions

- The company currently accepts relevant customer work in the market.
- The market has meaningful search or customer demand.
- The page targets a distinct local commercial intent.
- The company can fulfill the advertised service.
- The content includes market-specific value beyond city substitution.
- The page has a named owner and review cadence.
- Contact routing supports the market.
- Any linked profile relationship is accurate.

### Strong supporting evidence

- Approved local project experience
- Market-specific service constraints
- Verified dispatch or travel model
- Local customer or partner evidence
- Local technology or vehicle context
- Original market photography
- A staffed eligible location
- Recurring work or documented capacity

### Insufficient evidence by itself

- A city appears in a keyword tool.
- A technician lives nearby.
- A single temporary project occurred there.
- A competitor ranks there.
- The company could theoretically travel there.
- A job board permits targeting the city.

## 15. Recruiting Market Strategy

Recruiting geography is managed through job and candidate content, not local business profiles.

Each real opening must state:

- actual role title;
- genuine job location or approved territory;
- work-location model;
- travel expectations;
- engagement type after legal approval;
- qualifications;
- compensation when required or approved;
- application steps;

- publication and expiration dates.

Use `JobPosting` structured data only on an individual active job page that follows current Google requirements.

### Evergreen recruiting market pages

Do not create a broad `/careers/locations/[city]/` program at launch. Consider an evergreen market page only when:

- recurring recruiting demand is documented;
- the page does not imply a current opening;
- market-specific candidate information is substantial;
- the installer-network pathway is appropriate;
- ownership and review cadence are assigned; and
- the page is distinct from any commercial service-area page.

### Recruitment profile boundaries

- Do not change the primary Google Business Profile category to target installer candidates.
- Do not create Google Business Profiles for recruiters, job territories, or installer homes.
- Do not request customer reviews from applicants, employees, or contractors about recruiting experiences on the commercial profile.
- Use recruitment platforms, job pages, social channels, technical schools, trade groups, and referral programs for candidate distribution.

## 19. Review and Reputation Strategy

### 19.1 Eligible reviewers

Request customer reviews only from people or organizations with genuine customer experiences. Do not use employees, applicants, contractors, family members, vendors, or conflicted parties to influence the commercial rating.

### 19.2 Request workflow

An approved customer review workflow may:

1. Confirm that a genuine service interaction is complete.
2. Send the same neutral review request to eligible customers.
3. Link directly to the official review form.
4. Allow the customer to choose whether and what to write.
5. Record delivery, click, and response status without pressuring the customer.
6. Stop repeat requests after the approved limit.

### 19.3 Prohibited practices

- Review gating
- Incentives, discounts, gifts, or payment for reviews
- Requests for a specific star rating
- Requests for specific keywords or employee names
- Bulk requests disconnected from verified experiences
- Reviews written by staff or on behalf of customers
- Suppressing or discouraging negative reviews
- Moving recruiting testimonials into customer reviews

### 19.4 Response standard

- Respond professionally and promptly.
- Personalize the reply to the actual feedback.
- Protect customer, vehicle, project, and location privacy.
- Do not disclose contractual or operational details.
- Move sensitive resolution discussions to a private channel.
- Avoid keyword-stuffed or promotional replies.
- Escalate safety, legal, fraud, and privacy issues.

### 19.5 Review learning loop

Aggregate recurring themes to improve:

- service pages;
- FAQs;
- process explanations;
- sales messaging;
- scheduling communication;
- technician quality controls; and
- customer-experience workflows.

Never alter quoted review text when using an approved testimonial. Record permission and source.

## 22. Structured Data for Local Search

### Organization-level markup

Use `Organization` on the canonical organization page or another approved global location. Include only verified fields such as:

- name;
- URL;
- logo;
- contact points;
- legal name when appropriate; and
- controlled `sameAs` profiles.

### LocalBusiness markup

Use `LocalBusiness` only when an eligible business location exists and the visible page supports the same facts.

Potential properties include:

- `name`;
- `url`;
- `telephone`;
- `address`;
- `geo`;
- `openingHoursSpecification`;
- `image`;
- `areaServed`;
- `sameAs`; and
- the most accurate subtype available.

### Boundaries

- Do not add `LocalBusiness` to every page.
- Do not mark service areas as physical branches.
- Do not publish hidden addresses through schema when privacy or platform rules require them to remain private.
- Do not add aggregate ratings or reviews the site is not eligible to mark up.
- Do not create schema-only locations.
- Validate syntax and factual consistency before deployment.

Job pages follow the separate `JobPosting` specification.

## 24. AI, AEO, and Local Answer Visibility

No separate optimization method guarantees visibility in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, AI Overviews, or other answer systems. Build machine-understandable local authority through standard search fundamentals and clear evidence.

### Content pattern

- Answer the local question directly near the top.
- State whether the market is a location, service area, recruiting territory, or project area.
- Use concise factual summaries.
- Define services and installer roles clearly.
- Explain limitations and eligibility.
- Connect claims to visible proof.
- Use descriptive headings and internal links.
- Maintain consistent entity information.
- Keep important content crawlable without user interaction.
- Add eligible structured data.

### Citation-worthiness

Content is more useful to answer systems when it contains unique, current, attributable information such as:

- verified operating processes;
- clear job requirements;
- market-specific logistics;
- first-party project insights;
- original data or checklists;
- named review dates; and
- accountable authors or reviewers.

## 25. Featured Snippet and PAA Opportunities

Use concise answer formats for high-intent local questions.

### Paragraph answers

Best for definitions, eligibility, service-area explanations, and role summaries.

### Ordered lists

Best for application steps, fleet installation preparation, location verification, and project workflows.

### Tables

Best for role comparisons, service availability, market distinctions, and qualification summaries.

### FAQ blocks

Best for genuine user questions. FAQ markup does not guarantee a rich result and should only be used when current eligibility requirements are met.

Potential questions include:

- Does SB Mobile Installations serve my market?
- Does SB Mobile Installations have an office in this city?
- Where do mobile installers perform the work?
- Are installer jobs available near me?
- How much travel does a mobile installer role require?
- Can SB Mobile Installations coordinate multi-location fleet deployments?

Answers must reflect current verified operations.

## 29. Local KPI Framework

### Recruitment outcomes

- Organic impressions for role-plus-market queries
- Active job page clicks
- Qualified organic sessions to recruiting pages
- Application starts and completions
- Qualified applicant rate by market
- Cost per qualified applicant by distribution channel
- Interview, approval, and activation rate
- Time to fill or activate

### Commercial outcomes

- Local nonbrand impressions and clicks
- Google Business Profile website, call, and direction actions where applicable
- Market-page engaged sessions
- Qualified commercial inquiries
- Inquiry-to-opportunity rate
- Opportunity value influenced by organic and maps discovery
- Review volume, recency, response rate, and sentiment themes

### Quality controls

- Profile accuracy rate
- Citation consistency rate
- Duplicate-listing count
- Expired job URL removal time
- Holiday-hours accuracy
- Unanswered review and question count
- Location-page freshness compliance
- Unsupported geographic claims found

Rank tracking may support diagnosis but must not replace business outcomes. Results vary by searcher location, device, query, and personalization.

## 34. Implementation Phases

### Phase 0: Business verification

- Resolve legal, public, commercial, and hiring entities.
- Verify phone, address, hours, service model, and coverage.
- Identify profile owners and existing listings.
- Classify every known market.
- Establish approval owners.

### Phase 1: Entity foundation

- Create the canonical business-data record.
- Correct core website contact and organization information.
- Implement approved organization schema.
- Establish account ownership and secure access.
- Create the listing and citation inventory.

### Phase 2: Primary platform accuracy

- Claim or correct eligible Google Business Profile records.
- Claim or correct Bing Places records.
- Claim or correct Apple Maps records.
- Resolve duplicates and mismatches.
- Add tracking and verify destination pages.

### Phase 3: Recruitment-local foundation

- Publish the careers hub and active jobs index.
- Launch verified job pages with location accuracy.
- Implement job expiration and removal controls.
- Connect recruitment platforms to canonical job URLs.
- Track candidate source and quality.

### Phase 4: Commercial market authority

- Publish only approved market pages.
- Add market-specific proof and FAQs.
- Build relevant local and industry citations.
- Earn relationship-based links.
- Connect eligible profile URLs to the best local destination.

### Phase 5: Reputation and engagement

- Launch a policy-compliant customer review workflow.
- Establish response service levels.
- Add authentic media and updates.
- Use recurring questions and feedback to improve content.

### Phase 6: Expansion

- Evaluate new markets through demand, capacity, proof, and conversion data.
- Add profiles only for newly eligible locations.
- Add evergreen recruiting-market pages only where recurring demand justifies them.
- Consolidate or retire weak, outdated, or unsupported pages.

## 36. Prohibited Practices

- Keyword-stuffed business names
- Fake, rented, virtual, mailbox, or temporary locations
- Profiles for technician homes or customer sites
- Profiles for recruiting territories
- Duplicate profiles for the same eligible business
- Location pages created through city-name substitution
- Footer city lists designed primarily for rankings
- Hidden local text or links
- False national or local coverage claims
- Review gating or incentivized reviews
- Employee, applicant, or contractor reviews used to manipulate commercial ratings
- Fake questions, answers, photos, or check-ins
- `LocalBusiness` markup for nonexistent locations
- Structured ratings the website is not eligible to display
- Buying low-quality citations or links at scale
- Redirecting profile visitors to irrelevant pages
- Combining applicant and customer data without purpose and consent
- Keeping expired job pages live as active opportunities

## 37. Risk Register

| Risk | Impact | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Unverified Goodyear association published as fact | Profile suspension and trust loss | Treat as evidence only until confirmed |
| Wrong entity represented | Legal, recruiting, and knowledge-system confusion | Approve public, legal, and hiring entities |
| Service area confused with office | Misleading users and platform violations | Use geographic taxonomy and address rules |
| Recruiting market confused with commercial coverage | Wrong leads and unsupported claims | Separate page families and registries |
| Duplicate profile created | Ranking fragmentation and ownership conflict | Complete discovery audit first |
| Keyword-stuffed profile edits | Suspension or reduced trust | Require approval and policy review |
| Review gating | Review removal or profile restriction | Neutral all-customer request process |
| Thin city-page expansion | Indexation waste and weak quality | Enforce eligibility and distinct-content tests |
| Closed jobs remain visible | Candidate harm and job-search policy risk | Automated expiration and manual QA |
| Call tracking creates NAP inconsistency | Entity confusion | Preserve canonical number and controlled tracking |
| Platform ownership tied to one person | Loss of access | Business-owned accounts and multiple admins |
| Private project information exposed | Customer and contractual harm | Proof approval and privacy review |
