## 1. Purpose

This document provides a practical strategic overview of SB Mobile Installations for use in website planning, content development, recruiting, search optimization, design, and conversion strategy.

It defines:

- what the company appears to do;
- how its operating model should be explained;
- who the website must serve;
- which services and technical capabilities require confirmation;
- how the company may be positioned to installer candidates and commercial prospects;
- what differentiators can be developed with evidence;
- how recruitment and commercial growth support one another; and
- which business facts remain unresolved.

This document does not replace `01-business-source-of-truth.md`. The source-of-truth file controls factual approval. This overview converts approved, observed, inferred, and proposed information into a strategic working model.

## 2. Executive Business Summary

SB Mobile Installations appears to provide mobile installation and related field services for electronic equipment used in fleet, commercial, and construction vehicles.

Existing website and recruiting content indicate experience involving:

- GPS tracking equipment;
- electronic logging devices;
- fleet-management equipment;
- telematics hardware;
- vehicle camera systems;
- tire-pressure monitoring equipment;
- vehicle electrical systems;
- equipment testing;
- installation documentation; and
- mobile, on-site service.

The business appears to coordinate technicians who travel to customer or shop locations, complete installations using defined procedures, test installed equipment, document the work, and communicate with scheduling personnel.

The company’s immediate growth priority is not merely to generate more website traffic. It is to attract more qualified installer candidates and convert them into screened, onboarded, and activated installers.

The website should therefore operate as both:

1. A recruitment platform that explains opportunities, requirements, assignments, and application steps.
2. A commercial credibility platform that demonstrates service capability to fleet operators, technology providers, project partners, and other prospective customers.

The recruiting objective is primary. Commercial content remains important because qualified candidates are more likely to apply when the company appears credible, organized, technically capable, and active.

## 4. Working Business Model

### 4.1 Core operating model

The working business model is a distributed mobile installation organization that coordinates qualified field technicians to install electronic equipment in customer vehicles or at designated service locations.

The company may operate through a combination of:

- direct commercial relationships;
- equipment-provider relationships;
- fleet-management or telematics partners;
- project-based installation assignments;
- regional technician coverage;
- traveling technicians; and
- employee, contractor, or subcontractor labor.

Each element requires confirmation before it is presented as an established company fact.

### 4.2 Working service process

Based on existing recruiting content, a typical installation workflow may include:

1. A customer, provider, or project coordinator defines the installation requirement.
2. SB Mobile Installations schedules an approved technician.
3. Equipment, installation instructions, and approved connection procedures are provided.
4. The technician travels to the customer, fleet, yard, or shop location.
5. The technician gains appropriate access to the vehicle and installs the equipment.
6. The installed device is tested for functionality.
7. Required photographs, serial numbers, forms, or installation records are submitted.
8. Equipment and materials are accounted for.
9. Issues are escalated through the approved process.
10. The project or assignment is closed out.

This process is inferred and must be confirmed before it becomes customer-facing copy.

### 4.3 Revenue model requiring confirmation

Potential revenue sources may include:

- per-device installation fees;
- per-vehicle installation fees;
- project-based pricing;
- regional or multi-location deployment fees;
- travel charges;
- service, troubleshooting, or replacement work;
- subcontracted field services; and
- ongoing provider or fleet relationships.

Pricing methods, minimums, travel charges, margins, payment terms, and customer contract structures are not yet approved.

## 5. Recruitment as the Primary Growth Objective

The central business challenge is installer capacity. Commercial growth can be constrained when the company does not have enough qualified technicians in the right markets at the right time.

The new website should help the company:

- reach installers searching for relevant opportunities;
- explain the work before a recruiter invests time in a conversation;
- distinguish active jobs from future-work registration;
- qualify applicants through relevant experience and availability questions;
- improve mobile application completion;
- build installer coverage in priority markets;
- reduce dependence on a single job board;
- standardize recruiting messages across channels;
- nurture qualified candidates when no immediate opening exists;
- shorten application-to-contact time; and
- measure which sources produce activated installers.

### 5.1 Recruitment conversion definition

The desired conversion is not simply a submitted form.

The full recruitment conversion path is:

1. Candidate discovers SB Mobile Installations.
2. Candidate evaluates company and opportunity credibility.
3. Candidate reviews the work and requirements.
4. Candidate views a real opening or joins the installer network.
5. Candidate submits a complete first-step application.
6. Recruiting reviews and qualifies the application.
7. Candidate completes the required screening process.
8. Candidate completes documentation and onboarding.
9. Qualified installer becomes available or activated for appropriate work.

The primary performance measure should be the number and cost of qualified activated installers, not raw application volume.

## 6. Primary Candidate Audiences

### 6.1 Experienced telematics installers

These candidates may already have experience installing GPS, ELD, fleet-management, vehicle-camera, gateway, tracking, or connected-fleet equipment.

Likely candidate priorities include:

- clear equipment and project expectations;
- compensation clarity;
- territory and travel requirements;
- scheduling and assignment volume;
- payment timing;
- equipment and material coordination;
- documentation requirements;
- technical support; and
- repeat-work potential.

### 6.2 12-volt and mobile-electronics technicians

This audience may come from automotive electronics, alarms, stereos, accessories, upfitting, or related vehicle-electrical work.

The website should connect transferable skills to fleet technology installation without implying that every candidate automatically qualifies.

### 6.3 Fleet and commercial-vehicle technicians

These candidates may understand commercial vehicle environments, fleet operations, maintenance facilities, driver schedules, safety requirements, and field documentation.

They may need a clearer explanation of how technology installation differs from general maintenance or mechanical repair.

### 6.4 Field-service and adjacent technical workers

Candidates from low-voltage, telecommunications, electronics, equipment service, and related mobile technical roles may possess valuable diagnostic, routing, wiring, documentation, and customer-service skills.

Role content should identify which adjacent skills are acceptable and which vehicle-specific competencies are mandatory.

### 6.5 Emerging technicians

Technical-school graduates, veterans, apprentices, and early-career technicians may support long-term recruiting capacity only if the company has a genuine training, supervision, and qualification path.

The website must not imply entry-level eligibility or paid training unless those opportunities are verified.

## 7. Candidate Needs and Decision Factors

Qualified candidates are likely to evaluate the company based on questions such as:

- Is this a real and current opportunity?
- Which company would hire or contract with me?
- Is the role employee, contractor, subcontractor, temporary, or project based?
- Where is the work located?
- How much travel is required?
- What experience is mandatory?
- What tools, vehicle, smartphone, insurance, or licenses are required?
- Which equipment or platforms will I install?
- How is compensation calculated and when is it paid?
- Are travel, lodging, mileage, or expenses covered?
- How are assignments offered and scheduled?
- Is there a minimum amount of work?
- What documentation must I submit?
- Is training available?
- What happens after I apply?
- How quickly will someone contact me?

The careers experience should answer approved versions of these questions directly. Avoiding practical details may increase application volume but reduce candidate quality and trust.
